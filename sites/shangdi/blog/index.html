Name and Meaning (Etymology)

The name means "Highest Emperor; the supreme god of early Chinese religion" and reflects the figure's role in the tradition. Shàngdì, the Supreme Lord, was the high god of the Shang and early Zhou dynasties. He received sacrifices from the Son of Heaven and controlled harvest, warfare, and the weather. Later Chinese religion absorbed Shàngdì into the more bureaucratic Tiāndào, but the name retained its aura of absolute, personal sovereignty. Oracle bones ask whether Shàngdì will grant rain, bless the harvest, or approve a military campaign, revealing a direct, petitionary relationship between king and high god.
