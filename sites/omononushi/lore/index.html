05

Great Thing Lord of Mount Miwa

Mountain, Oracle, and Sacred Snake

Ōmononushi is the mysterious kami of Mount Miwa, one of the oldest and most enigmatic deities in Japan. He is associated with snakes, oracles, and the transfer of the land to Amaterasu's descendants. Unlike most kami, he had no image and was worshipped in the form of the mountain itself.

Mountain God

He is identified with Mount Miwa itself, worshipped without an image.

Snake Deity

He appears in legends as a serpent who tests and guides humans.

Oracle Kami

His will was sought through divination at Ōmiwa Shrine.

Land Transfer

He is linked to the myth of ceding the land to the heavenly descendants.

Sacred Symbols

Mount Miwa The mountain itself, worshipped as the kami's body
Snake The form in which Ōmononushi appears in legends
Sake Offered to the kami and associated with his serpent manifestation
Mirror Used in divination and ritual at Ōmiwa Shrine
06

Mythology

Stories of Omononushi

Ōmononushi's mythology is preserved in strange, archaic tales of snakes, possession, and oracles.

Kojiki

The Serpent Husband

A beautiful woman named Ikutamayori was visited at night by a mysterious lover. She became pregnant, and her parents asked her to identify him. She placed a needle threaded with red silk in his garment and followed the thread in the morning. It led to Mount Miwa and disappeared into a cave. The kami revealed himself as Ōmononushi, a great serpent, and asked to be worshipped. The child born from this union became an ancestor of the Miwa priestly line.

Nihon Shoki

Oracle of the Land

When the heavenly descendants sought to take possession of the land, Ōmononushi — identified here with Ōkuninushi — was consulted. He declared that the land should be surrendered peacefully and withdrew to a hidden realm. His oracle made the transfer of sovereignty possible without war.

Cult

Worship without Image

Ōmiwa Shrine is one of Japan's oldest shrines and has no honden (main hall) housing an image. The mountain itself is the kami, and worship is directed toward its peak. This aniconic practice preserves an ancient stratum of Japanese religion.

Extended Lore

Ōmononushi is the god who has no face because the mountain is his face. He reminds us that the sacred can be a place rather than a person, and that some deities are worshipped best by looking up at a peak rather than into an image.
