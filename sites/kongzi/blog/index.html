Name and Meaning (Etymology)

The name means "Master Kong" and reflects the figure's role in the tradition. Kǒngzǐ, or Confucius, is the Spring-and-Autumn period teacher whose sayings were compiled in the Analects. He advocated rén (humaneness), lǐ (ritual propriety), and xiào (filial piety) as the foundations of a harmonious society. Later dynasties elevated him to the status of culture-hero and patron of education, though he presented himself as a transmitter rather than an innovator. The Analects preserve his dialogues with disciples on governance, learning, and ritual, forming one of the most influential educational canons in world history.
