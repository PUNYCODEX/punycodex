Name and Meaning (Etymology)

The name means "Jade Emperor" and reflects the figure's role in the tradition. Yùhuáng, the Jade Emperor, is the supreme ruler of the Daoist celestial bureaucracy. He presides over the Three Realms—heaven, earth, and the underworld—and judges the dead at the Chinese New Year. His authority is administrative rather than creative; he delegates cosmic functions to a vast pantheon of ministers and generals. His birthday, celebrated on the ninth day of the first lunar month, involves offerings of incense, paper money, and vegetarian food across Chinese communities.
