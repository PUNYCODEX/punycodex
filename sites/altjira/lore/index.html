The Authentic Name

Unicode restoration and ASCII comparison

Scholarly Transliteration

Altjira

The name survives in scholarly transliteration. Altjira is the standard Aboriginal romanisation, documented in academic sources — “The dreamtime”. Because the spelling uses only Latin letters, the form is the same in both ASCII and Unicode.

No indigenous writing system is securely attested for individual aboriginal names. The form shown is a modern scholarly transliteration.

ASCII Constraint

altjira

The plain altjira form is identical to the Unicode restoration. Because this name is already written in Latin letters, no diacritics, stress, or script information were lost — only capitalization differs.

Unicode Restoration

Altjira

The Unicode restoration does not need to recover lost marks for Altjira. Its value is canonical spelling and consistent cataloguing, not the reconstruction of erased orthography. The domain is readable as-is to both DNS and humanity.

Pronunciation

How Altjira was spoken

/ˈaltjɪrə/ Arrernte Reconstruction
Alt- Stressed first syllable [alt], with the voiceless lateral cluster typical of Arrernte orthography; the lateral 'l' is followed by a palatalised release into 'tj'.
-ji- High front vowel [i] preceded by the lamino-palatal affricate 'tj' [c], a sound common in Central Australian languages.
-ra Final syllable [ɾə], with a tapped 'r' and a reduced vowel in unstressed position.

Modern Approximation

'AHLT-chi-ruh' — stress the first syllable, pronounce the 'tj' as the 'ch' in 'church', and let the final syllable relax.

Etymological Kin

  • Arrernte Altjira, the eternal sky being of the Arrernte Dreaming
  • English rendering Alchera / Alcheringa, the Dreaming, popularised by Spencer and Gillen

The Accent / Script Rule

The name is Arrernte, not Greek or Sanskrit, so it has no 'original script' in the classical sense. The ASCII form altjira flattens the distinctive palatal cluster and removes the capital that marks a proper name; the restoration Altjira is the scholarly citation form.

05

The Eternal Sky Being

The Dreaming, Creation, and the Sky Above

Altjira is the eternal sky being of the Arrernte peoples of Central Australia. In the early ethnographic record he is described as the uncreated creator: he made the earth, shaped its first features, and then withdrew to dwell forever in the sky. His feet are said to be like those of an emu, and he never needs to bend, because the sky is high and he is eternal. Behind the figure lies the deeper Arrernte concept of altjira — the Dreaming — the timeless ground of law, country, and ancestral identity.

The Eternal Sky

Altjira dwells in the sky above, unborn and undying, the source from which the visible world was shaped.

The Dreaming

Altjira names the timeless epoch in which ancestral beings travelled, made country, and left law for the living.

Creation of the Earth

He formed the land, its waterholes and ranges, and then ascended to the sky, leaving the world for his children.

Ancestral Law

The stories and ceremonies that bind people to country are carried as sacred trust by Arrernte custodians.

Sacred Symbols

The sky The eternal canopy where Altjira dwells and from which creation descended.
Emu footprints His feet are likened to those of an emu, a sign of his other-than-human nature and his connection to the Australian land.
The Milky Way In Arrernte cosmology the sky river is camp and road for ancestral beings, linking earth and heaven.
Waterholes and ranges Features of country created during the Dreaming and maintained through ceremony today.
06

Mythology

Stories of Altjira

The mythology of Altjira comes to us through Arrernte oral tradition and the early ethnographies of Spencer and Gillen, Carl Strehlow, and later scholars. The stories are not entertainment; they are title deeds to country, instructions for ceremony, and maps of moral responsibility. Some details are held by initiated custodians and are not shared in public space.

Arrernte cosmogony

The Uncreated Creator

In the account recorded by Spencer and Gillen, Altjira is eternal: he was not born and will not die. In the beginning he made the earth and all that it contains — its hills, waterholes, plants, and animals — and then he rose into the sky, where he remains with his two sons. The earth was left for the ancestors who followed and for the human beings who would inherit their law.

Arrernte cosmogony

The Sky Above

Altjira lives in the sky, high above the camps of men and women. His feet are like the feet of the emu, and he never bends them, because the sky is wide and there is no obstacle in his path. The stars and the Milky Way are the camps and tracks of ancestral beings, so that the night sky is read as a mirror of the country below.

The Dreaming

Alcheringa

The term alcheringa — rendered from Arrernte altjira — names the Dreaming: the time when the ancestors moved across the land, creating its forms and leaving behind the songs, designs, and ceremonies that maintain it. Altjira is both the name of the eternal sky being and the name of the epoch in which the world received its present shape. Later Arrernte scholarship, especially T. G. H. Strehlow, stressed that altjira primarily means 'to dream' and refers to the eternal creative order rather than to a single personal deity.

Living tradition

Custodianship

The public, scholarly record of Altjira is only one layer of knowledge. Arrernte custodians hold song-cycles, designs, and ceremonial responsibilities that are not published and are transmitted under strict traditional protocols. PuniCodex presents only the documented, open scholarly account and acknowledges that deeper knowledge belongs to the communities who keep it.

Extended Lore

Altjira asks us to think about time differently. The Dreaming is not a long-ago past; it is the continuing ground of the present. The ancestors did their work, but the work is not finished — it is renewed every time a song is sung, a design is painted, or a custodian walks country.
